# Current Patient Journey and Workflow Gaps

## SIH Problem Statement ID
26133

## Project
Accessibility and Quality of Public Healthcare Services in Rural and Underserved Areas

---

## 1. Purpose

This document maps the current healthcare journey of a patient in rural and underserved areas and identifies the major workflow gaps, delays and continuity problems that can occur during the journey.

The analysis follows the patient from the community level through primary healthcare facilities and, where required, to higher-level hospitals and specialist care.

---

## 2. Current Patient Journey Overview

The typical healthcare journey may involve the following stages:

Patient
→ Frontline Healthcare Worker / ASHA / ANM
→ Sub-centre or Primary Health Centre (PHC)
→ Doctor Consultation
→ Rural Hospital, if required
→ District Hospital or Specialist Care, if required
→ Follow-up

Each stage may involve different healthcare workers, facilities and information flows. Gaps between these stages can affect timely access and continuity of care.

## 4. Major Workflow Gaps Identified

The current patient journey reveals several major gaps that affect access, continuity and quality of healthcare services:

1. **Information discontinuity:** Patient information may not move consistently from frontline healthcare workers to PHCs and higher-level hospitals.

2. **Repeated information collection:** Patients may need to repeatedly provide the same medical information at different healthcare facilities.

3. **Travel and access delays:** Patients may need to travel long distances before reaching appropriate healthcare services or specialists.

4. **Consultation delays:** Staff shortages and high patient load can increase waiting time for consultation.

5. **Fragmented referral process:** Referrals may not be consistently tracked from creation to completion.

6. **Limited visibility of patient progress:** Healthcare workers may find it difficult to monitor the patient's journey across multiple facilities.

7. **Diagnostic coordination gaps:** Required tests and diagnostic information may not be coordinated efficiently.

8. **Missed follow-ups:** High-risk and chronic-condition patients may miss required follow-up care.

9. **Limited resource visibility:** Information about medicine and healthcare services may not always be available when needed.

10. **Limited administrative monitoring:** Healthcare administrators may lack a unified view of patient flow, referrals, follow-ups and healthcare service quality.

---

## 5. Opportunity for the Proposed Solution

The identified workflow gaps show that the proposed solution should improve coordination across the complete patient healthcare journey rather than focus on only one stage of healthcare.

The solution should create a connected workflow where relevant patient information can move with the patient from the community level to primary healthcare facilities and, when required, to higher-level hospitals and specialist care.

The proposed system should focus on:

1. Digital patient registration and longitudinal patient records.
2. Assisted symptom and basic health information collection.
3. Digital triage and patient prioritization.
4. Appointment and queue management.
5. Improved consultation coordination.
6. Closed-loop referral creation and tracking.
7. Diagnostic coordination and information visibility.
8. Medicine availability visibility.
9. High-risk patient identification and follow-up tracking.
10. Facility-level dashboards for monitoring healthcare services.

The overall objective is to reduce delays and unnecessary travel, improve continuity of care and strengthen coordination between patients, frontline healthcare workers and different levels of the existing public healthcare system.

---

## 6. Conclusion

The analysis of the current patient journey demonstrates that healthcare access challenges in rural and underserved areas are closely connected across multiple stages of care.

A patient may face delays from the moment they first seek help, during travel to a healthcare facility, while waiting for consultation, during referral to higher-level facilities and during follow-up after treatment.

The major challenge is therefore not only access to a doctor or hospital. It is the lack of a connected and coordinated healthcare journey.

The proposed solution should address these workflow gaps by supporting continuity of patient information, timely patient prioritization, improved coordination, referral tracking and follow-up management.

By connecting the different stages of the healthcare journey, the system can help reduce unnecessary delays, improve healthcare accessibility and strengthen the existing public healthcare ecosystem.
